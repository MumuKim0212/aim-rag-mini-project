# 001c: Chunk size 900 / 1000 / 1100 (follow-up of 001b)

## 1. Hypothesis

- Change (one variable): Recursive chunk size 1000/125 and 1100/138 (overlap = size/8, rounded) against the baseline 800/100; 900/112 was measured in card 001b and is not rerun. Requested by the project owner on 2026-10-21: compare 900, 1000, 1100 and take the highest.

- Expected effect: no prediction beyond "somewhere between 0.75 and 0.77"; the curve was not monotonic at 500–900, so a neighbour of 900 can also be lower.

- Baseline run (reused): `dev_000_baseline`. Candidates: 900 (001b), 1000, 1100; the other sizes are shown for context.

- This is the **last chunk-size sweep**: no further sizes are added after seeing these results (the winner of a growing candidate set is increasingly a chance pick on 74 questions).
- Not looked at: test split, self-written golden set.

## 3. Go/no-go criteria (written before results)

- Selection rule (same bars as card 001b): among 900, 1000, 1100 take those with mean `evid@5` ≥ baseline + 0.03 (≥ 0.727), (higher − lower) ≥ 5 questions, and context ≤ 1.5× baseline (≤ 4,596). The highest `evid@5` wins; within 0.01 of the highest counts as tied and the shortest context wins. None qualifies → baseline 800/100 stays.
- The overall best chunk size of cards 001–001c is the winner of this rule if it is among 900/1000/1100, since all of them beat the sizes measured before (1200: 0.748).
- Headroom gate for the winner: `evalkit.headroom --metric evid@5 --noise 14`, upside − risk > 14; if it fails, no answer run and the adopted size stays 800/100 unless the project owner waives the gate in writing (date and name in section 7).
- Answer-run adopt rule and cost cap: same as card 001.

## 7. Decision and notes

- Decision: **900/112 is the best chunk size at the retrieval level (cards 001–001c); the adopted size stays 800/100 until the headroom gate is waived in writing.** The size sweep is closed; no more sizes are tested.
- Why, with question IDs: `evid@5` by size: 400 0.675, 500 0.659, 700 0.714, 800 0.697, 900 0.771, 1000 0.759, 1100 0.741, 1200 0.748. 1000 and 1100 keep slightly more questions with all evidence (43 vs 42) but lose 8 questions each against 4 for 900 (shared losers of the larger sizes: ADD-HF05 ADD-HF07 ADD-HF06 ADD-SE08 ADD-IN08 POS045), and their contexts are 8% and 16% longer. Beyond 900 the curve flattens or falls.
- What the criteria missed, if anything: nothing was changed after the results. The differences between 900, 1000 and 1200 are 1–2 points of mean `evid@5`, i.e. a few questions; the evidence supports "900 to 1200 are similar and better than 800 at retrieval level", not a sharp optimum at 900.
